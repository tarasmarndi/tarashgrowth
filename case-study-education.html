Education lead generation strategy

An anonymised example of audience segmentation, creative planning and lead-generation campaign structure for education admissions.

Anonymised projectMeta AdsStrategy & execution
Confidentiality note: This case study intentionally omits client identity, account screenshots, customer information and non-public performance data. Replace prompts with details you are authorised to share. Do not publish illustrative figures as actual results.

01. Project overview

The project focused on planning a digital admissions approach for an educational institution serving multiple age and class groups.

  • Industry: Education
  • Objective: Generate relevant admissions enquiries and build local awareness
  • My contribution: Audience research, segmentation approach, creative strategy and campaign planning

02. The challenge

Different admission stages have different parent concerns, information needs and decision timelines. A single message would not speak equally well to every audience.

03. Research & planning

  • Group audiences by admission stage and likely parent needs.
  • Map common questions and decision barriers for each segment.
  • Review the information available on the relevant landing pages.

04. Strategy & execution

  • Separate messaging for pre-primary, primary and middle-school audiences.
  • Develop creative angles around learning environment, development and academic progression.
  • Align campaign objective, audience, creative and lead form with the intended enquiry journey.

05. Measurement & learning

Evaluate delivery, reach, frequency, CTR, form completion, cost per lead and lead quality together. A low CPL alone does not establish campaign success.

Results: Add only approved, verifiable results. If results cannot be shared, state: “Performance data withheld due to client confidentiality.” If this was planning work rather than a launched campaign, label it as a strategy project.

06. My key takeaways

Audience segmentation is most useful when it changes the message and experience, not merely the names of ad sets.
