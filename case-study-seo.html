SEO research & optimisation

A structured SEO research process for connecting keyword opportunities with search intent, useful website pages and measurable organic visibility.

01. Project overview

This case study outlines an SEO research and optimisation workflow covering keyword discovery, intent classification and page-level recommendations for a service-focused website.

  • Industry: Website / service business
  • Objective: Improve relevance between target search queries and useful website content
  • My contribution: Keyword research, intent mapping, page review and optimisation recommendations

02. The challenge

A keyword list by itself does not create an SEO strategy. Search terms need to be grouped by intent and mapped to pages that genuinely answer the searcher's question or help them take the next step.

03. Research & planning

  • Started with core service and topic terms, then expanded the list with relevant variations.
  • Grouped keywords by topic and classified their likely search intent.
  • Reviewed existing page topics, titles, headings and content relevance to identify gaps and overlap.

04. Strategy & execution

  • Clustered related search terms into focused keyword groups.
  • Mapped each group to an appropriate existing or proposed landing page.
  • Prioritised title tags, headings, page copy and internal-link recommendations.
  • Separated content recommendations from technical issues that require additional validation.

05. Measurement & learning

Use Google Search Console to monitor impressions, clicks, CTR and average position for relevant query and page groups. Where analytics tracking is configured, connect organic sessions to meaningful enquiries or other site conversions.

Results: This page describes the research and recommendation framework. No ranking, traffic or conversion uplift is claimed without verified before-and-after data.

06. My key takeaways

Good keyword research starts with understanding the searcher. Relevance, page usefulness and a clear measurement plan matter more than adding keywords for their own sake.
